## Context

Flight-control cable pulleys and their guide brackets carry the control cables that run through the airframe. My work was designing these parts to new dimensions in **Creo and SolidWorks**, and producing the drawings that went to manufacturing.

## The DFM design fix

The parts as designed left sharp edges that had to be **deburred by hand** after machining — a manual step repeated on every part.

I added **edge radii** to the pulleys and brackets so that the finished geometry came off the machine without the edges that needed deburring. Removing that manual step **cut monthly production time by 8%**.

## Making the fix stick

A fix on today's parts only helps today's parts. I also **updated the legacy designs in the company's design library** with the same edge radii, so any future design started from the improved version instead of reintroducing the deburring step.

## Drawings and tolerances

I created **manufacturing drawings with GD&T** for machined, cast and sheet metal parts.

Before release to production I ran **tolerance stack-ups** on the assemblies, to catch fit problems on paper rather than at assembly — where they cost far more to fix.

## Outcome

- Pulleys and guide brackets designed to new dimensions in **Creo and SolidWorks**
- Manual deburring removed, cutting **monthly production time 8%**
- Legacy library designs updated so future parts start from the improved geometry
- GD&T drawings for machined, cast and sheet metal parts, checked with tolerance stack-ups before release

## What I carry forward

The biggest gain came from a small geometric change made with the shop floor in mind. Manufacturing time is decided at the design stage — and the library update is what turned one improvement into the default.
