## The problem

A research group was using **six separate instruments** in the field. Each one had to be carried, set up and powered on its own, which added gear and setup work to every field visit.

## The design

I designed a single **3D-printed field instrument** that brings the sensors, the battery and a fan together in one unit — **replacing all six instruments**.

Combining them meant planning the layout around how the unit would be built and used:

- every sensor placed where it can still take its reading
- the battery and fan packaged inside the same body
- parts designed to print and assemble cleanly, so revisions were quick

## Build, test, revise

3D printing let me check each version physically rather than only on screen — fit, assembly and how the unit handled in use. Findings went straight back into the CAD model for the next print.

As with every design in this role, I **tested and validated the unit with the requesting department before the final build**, and reviewed the design with the manufacturing and product development teams before fabrication.

## Outcome

- Six separate instruments replaced by **one 3D-printed unit**
- Sensors, battery and fan housed together
- Validated with the requesting department before final build

## Part of a wider role

This is one of **40+ custom products** I've designed and built for campus departments at Mississippi State, alongside work such as redesigned sample dryer racks (fixing warped frames, upgrading the mesh, adding insulated handles) and wall-mounted storage with adjustable dual-arm clamps that hold irregular tools off the floor.
