## The problem

A research group was using **six separate instruments** in the field. Each one had to be carried, set up and powered on its own, which added gear and setup work to every field visit.

## The design

I designed a single **3D-printed field instrument** that brought the sensors, the battery and a fan together in one unit — **replacing all six instruments**.

Combining them meant planning the layout around how the unit would be built and used:

- every sensor placed where it could still take its reading
- the battery and fan packaged inside the same body
- parts designed to print and assemble cleanly, so revisions were quick

## Build, test, revise

3D printing let me check each version physically rather than only on screen — fit, assembly and how the unit handled in use. Findings went straight back into the CAD model for the next print.

As with every design in this role, I **tested and validated the unit with the requesting department before final build**, and **reviewed the design with the campus machine shop and research staff before fabrication**.

## Outcome

- Six separate instruments replaced by **one 3D-printed unit**
- Sensors, battery and fan housed together
- Tested and validated with the requesting department before final build

## Part of a wider role

This was one of **40+ custom products** I designed and built for campus departments at Mississippi State in Creo, SolidWorks, and Siemens NX, including custom equipment and fixtures. Others included redesigned sample dryer racks (fixed warped frames, upgraded the mesh, and added insulated handles) and wall-mounted storage with adjustable dual-arm clamps that hold irregular tools off the floor.
