## The job

Each walk-in cold room was a custom build. The insulated wall, ceiling and door panels had to be made to that site's dimensions, the evaporator and condensing units were assembled from parts supplied by a sister plant, and the whole room was then installed at the client's site.

Over the year I worked on **42 of these cold rooms**, from panel fabrication through installation and after-sales service.

## Finding the bottleneck

On-site setup was taking **4 days per cold room**. Because every room ended in an installation at a client site, that setup time was paid 42 times over.

Working through the installations, I found the assembly bottleneck that was holding up setup. Fixing it meant coordinating on site with the local civil engineers, so their work and our assembly lined up instead of one waiting on the other.

The result: setup dropped to **3 days per cold room — a 25% reduction**.

## Keeping installs on schedule

Site work doesn't wait for missing parts. When components were out of stock, I **fabricated and installed replacements on site** with the team so the installation could continue on schedule instead of stopping for a delivery.

## Service and recurring defects

After installation, I handled **service calls** — troubleshooting and repairing panels, doors, evaporators and condensing units.

Where the same assembly defect kept coming back, I used **root cause analysis and 5-Why** to trace it to its source and fix it there, rather than repairing the same symptom on each job.

## Outcome

- **42 walk-in cold rooms** built and installed at client sites
- On-site setup cut by **25%**, from 4 days to 3 per room
- Installations kept on schedule by fabricating out-of-stock parts on site
- Recurring assembly defects fixed at the root with **RCA and 5-Why**

## What I carry forward

The improvement came from the field, not from a drawing. Being on site for the installations is what made the bottleneck visible, and fixing it meant working with people outside my own team.
